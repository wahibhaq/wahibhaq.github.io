## Why Design Jam should be more attended by developers and engineers

I attended a day long event on 22nd April known as "Design Jam" here in Munich. It was quite an amazing experience for me as a developer/engineer. Funny enough I was always shortlisted for past 3 years but never managed to actually attend it but finally (quite gladly!) managed this time. Just wanted to write a short post about the event and some of my learnings before I forget :) 

[Design Jam](http://designj.am/) is basically a one-day event that brings together Designers, Developers and Entrepreneurs, to solve a problem and build a prototype in just 10 hours (or so). Each Jam’s theme will be announced on the morning of, for maximum excitement. It was started by [Rachel](https://twitter.com/rilan) who is currently working as UX Designer on the Chrome UX team. You can check out her work on her personal [website](http://www.rachelilansimpson.com/).  

This time, theme was "How to innovate furniture building process using Microsoft HoloLens". In Europe/US, it's quite common to receive blocks of furniture which you have to assemble yourself and if you have done it yourself you'll agree that it's an extremely tedious/boring task. Every attendee had a good understanding of the problem because of IKEA furnitures which come with a paper manual. Dismantling a furniture item is a bigger problem and requires more care and attention. Coincidently, I was already planning to dismantle a wardrobe from friend's place and assemble it back in my apartment. Later, the Design Jam was somewhat helpful with mental preparation what to expect -_- 

Anyways coming back to event, it started with short intro about event and mandatory sponsorship message from [Vectorform](https://www.vectorform.com/). Vectorform is a pretty cool and innovative company here in Munich and by the way, they are looking for [kick-ass designers](https://vectorform.applicantpro.com/jobs/454506.html) and [business development manager](https://vectorform.applicantpro.com/jobs/464275.html). So after the intro session, they revealed the theme which was basically about finding innovative solutions to furniture assembly using Augmented Reality (AR) Technology. I am quite aware of how Vectorform closely works with AR and VR concepts so it wasn't a surprise for me. The recommended AR gadget was Microsoft HoloLens and we were also given a basic demo of it. The solutions shouldn't be limited to capabilities of HoloLens but it was meant to give a good idea of limitations and real-life usecase.





We were are a team of 3 including the business guy from Allianz and designer from IDEO. Frankly, it was first time for me to create storyboards but it was fun. My core learning was about how differently engineers and designers perceive a solution and we definitely suck (quite a stereotype) at sketching :) 

- background and inspiration to get started with.
